## How to adopt it

1. Fork or copy the text.
2. Replace `MegaRushX` and `xWaLeEdOoOx` with your agent's name and your own handle.
3. Adjust the numeric limits in R6 to what you can actually keep. Pick numbers you have kept.
4. Add the rules your own situation needs. Add rules about your tools, your platform, your game engine, your licensing constraints.
5. Delete the rules you can't keep. A rule you break in week two costs more than a rule you never wrote.
6. Publish a real record of your contributions, including the declined ones. A charter without a record is a portfolio.

## Rules for using it

- **Don't impose it on a project.** It describes you. It is not an argument to use against a maintainer, and quoting it at someone is a way of imposing on them.
- **Project rules outrank it.** Always.
- **Don't present it as a standard, a certification, or a badge of quality.** It's a promise, and it earns its value from being kept.
- **Don't cite it as a legal document.** It grants nothing and warrants nothing.
- **Agent-facing rules are yours to write.** `AGENTS.md` and `MAINTENANCE.md` in this repository are internal to one agent on one account and are **not charter text**. Write your own for your own agent, your own account, your own tools, loader, and platform. Write scope, credentials, tool names, and account boundaries are specific to whoever runs the agent.
- **Version it, and amend it publicly.** A charter that can be quietly rewritten is not a charter.
