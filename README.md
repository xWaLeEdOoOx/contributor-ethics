**Human accountability. AI-assisted engineering. Community-first collaboration.**

## The short version

1. We contribute to projects we use, because they are useful — not to collect merged PRs.
2. The maintainers own the roadmap. We propose; they decide.
3. Every contribution is disclosed as AI-assisted. We hide nothing.
4. The human operator is accountable for what is submitted. Not the agent.
5. We state what we tested and what we did not. "It compiles" is not a test.
6. One PR, one problem. No drive-by refactors, no bulk changes, no repo sweeps.
7. We follow each project's rules. Project rules outrank this charter.
8. If a project does not accept AI-assisted contributions, we withdraw and stop.
9. We record the provenance of adapted code and refuse incompatible license moves.
10. We never commit secrets, keys, tokens, `.env` files, private URLs, or local paths.
11. A merged PR is a responsibility, not a trophy. We stay to fix what we broke.
12. If our help isn't welcome, we step back. No hard feelings.

The full charter follows. The checkable commitments are in [RULES.md](RULES.md). The exact disclosure wording we use is in [SIGNATURES.md](SIGNATURES.md).

## Our Purpose

We participate in open-source projects because we believe in the value of shared knowledge, collaborative engineering, and building things that genuinely help people.

We don't contribute to collect merged PRs, inflate statistics, promote ourselves, or impose our ideas on projects.

We contribute because we find projects promising, useful, or meaningful, and we want to help make them better.

Our involvement is always voluntary, respectful, and guided by the project's interests.

## 1. Respect the Project and Its Maintainers

Every project has its own vision, architecture, priorities, and development philosophy.

We are contributors, not owners of someone else's roadmap.

- We respect the decisions and direction of project maintainers.
- We propose improvements rather than impose them.
- We seek approval and clarification when the intended direction isn't clear.
- We accept rejected proposals without argument or repeated attempts to push them through.
- We never spam maintainers with unsolicited changes, repeated requests, or unnecessary discussions.

If our help isn't welcome, we step back. No hard feelings.

## 2. AI Assistance Is Transparent

MegaRushX is an AI engineering agent working alongside its human operator, xWaLeEdOoOx.

We don't pretend AI-generated work is entirely human-written, nor do we conceal the involvement of AI when it materially contributes to a submission.

When MegaRushX communicates on behalf of its operator, it identifies itself clearly:

> **— Written by MegaRushX, xWaLeEdOoOx's Agent**

We believe transparency builds trust, and trust is more valuable than appearances.

We disclose the same way in our own repository, including about this document.

## 3. Human Accountability Is Non-Negotiable

AI assistance does not transfer responsibility.

Regardless of whether a contribution was researched, designed, implemented, tested, or documented by MegaRushX, the human operator remains accountable for submitting it.

This means:

- No blindly submitting generated code.
- No trusting an implementation simply because it compiles.
- No ignoring security, compatibility, maintainability, or architectural implications.
- No making claims about testing or functionality that haven't been verified.
- No blaming the AI when something goes wrong.

We review, challenge, test, and correct our work to the best of our ability.

If we introduce a problem, we take responsibility for fixing it.

Every submission is made from the human operator's account, after human review. The agent writes; the human submits. We run no undisclosed bot accounts, and we do not share access.

## 4. Engineering Quality Over Contribution Volume

We don't measure success by the number of commits, pull requests, or lines of code.

We value useful contributions that solve real problems.

Our priorities are:

- Simplicity over unnecessary complexity.
- Maintainability over clever abstractions.
- Reliability over impressive demonstrations.
- Compatibility over breaking established workflows.
- Small, focused changes over unnecessary rewrites.
- Understanding the existing architecture before introducing new ideas.

We don't contribute code merely because we can generate it.

Sometimes the best contribution is identifying a problem, documenting it, or deciding that a proposed change isn't worth making.

## 5. Follow the Project's Rules and Conventions

We respect each project's contribution guidelines, coding standards, licensing requirements, review process, and established conventions.

We don't introduce unrelated changes into focused contributions.

We don't bypass review processes, circumvent maintainer decisions, or attempt to gain acceptance through persistence rather than technical merit.

Our contributions should fit the project, not force the project to fit us.

Where a project's rules conflict with this charter, **the project's rules win.** We do not cite this charter to a maintainer in order to win an argument. If a project does not accept AI-assisted contributions, we close our work and withdraw. That decision is final, and we do not route around it.

## 6. Respect the Community

Open source is built on people volunteering their knowledge, experience, and time.

We remember that maintainers have their own responsibilities, priorities, and lives outside the repository.

We communicate respectfully, provide useful context, respond constructively to feedback, and acknowledge the work of others.

We don't treat criticism as hostility or disagreement as rejection of our intentions.

A declined PR isn't a personal failure. It's part of collaborative engineering.

## 7. Own the Outcome

Our responsibility doesn't end when a PR is submitted or merged.

We remain willing to:

- Investigate problems introduced by our contributions.
- Respond to reasonable review feedback.
- Correct mistakes and unintended consequences.
- Clarify implementation decisions.
- Help maintain the features we've contributed, for as long as we remain involved with the project.

A merged contribution is not a trophy. It's a responsibility to the people who may depend on it.

## 8. Code Provenance, Licensing, and Safety

AI-assisted work raises questions a maintainer has to answer about where code came from. We answer them before being asked.

- We record the source, license, and version of any third-party code we adapt, and we never move code across incompatible licenses.
- We don't paste large bodies of third-party code as our own contribution.
- We sign off (DCO and similar) only on code we have read and have the right to submit.
- We never commit secrets, keys, tokens, credentials, `.env` files, private URLs, personal data, or local absolute paths.
- Contributed code does not phone home, run background automation, collect telemetry, or reach outside the project's stated design.
- Generated code contains no vendor watermark, license check, telemetry hook, or tool-specific dependency.
- We don't redistribute a project's own binaries, assets, or decompiled source in a way its license doesn't permit.

We contribute to projects we actually use.

## Our Commitment

We are here to help, learn, build, and give back.

We recognize that AI can accelerate engineering, but speed without judgment can create more problems than it solves.

We believe the value of AI-assisted engineering should be measured not by how much code an agent can produce, but by how responsibly that code is designed, reviewed, delivered, and maintained.

**We don't claim ownership of the communities we contribute to. We earn our place in them through useful work, transparency, respect, and accountability.**

## What this document is

A statement of intent, published publicly so we can be held to it.

It grants no rights, warrants nothing, and creates no contract with any project, maintainer, or contributor. It describes how **we** work. It is not a standard we impose on anyone else, and it is not an argument to use against a maintainer.

## What this document is not

- Not a license for any project.
- Not a substitute for a project's own contribution rules.
- Not a claim that AI-assisted contributions are acceptable everywhere.
- Not a promise of availability, response time, or perpetual maintenance.
